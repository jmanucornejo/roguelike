Chasqui — relay messenger who ran along the Inca road network.
Quipucamayoc — specialist who created and interpreted quipus, the knotted recording system.
Amauta — teacher or learned scholar who educated nobles.
Haravicu — poet, singer, or composer who preserved stories and traditions.
Curaca — local community leader responsible for administration, labor, and tribute.
Willac Umu — the empire’s chief priest.
Aclla — selected woman trained in weaving, food preparation, and religious service.
Qollqa kamayuq — keeper or administrator of government storehouses.
Chacra kamayuq — agricultural supervisor responsible for fields and crops.
Llama michiq — llama or alpaca herder.
Mitmaq or mitimae — person relocated by the state to farm, govern, teach skills, or secure newly conquered territory.
Yana or yanacona — permanent servant or worker attached to the ruler, nobility, or temples.
Awqaq — warrior or soldier.
Runa simi kamayuq — interpreter, especially in territories with different languages.


Conquistador — soldier or expedition member involved in conquering Indigenous territories.
Encomendero — Spaniard granted the right to receive tribute or labor from an Indigenous community.
Corregidor — royal official who governed a district, collected taxes, and enforced colonial law.
Virrey — viceroy who governed a colony on behalf of the Spanish king.
Oidor — judge serving in a royal court called an Audiencia.
Escribano — notary or clerk who prepared contracts, wills, court records, and official documents.
Alguacil — law-enforcement officer, bailiff, or constable.
Visitador — royal inspector sent to investigate officials, taxes, or local administration.
Doctrinero — priest responsible for teaching Christianity to Indigenous communities.
Fraile — friar belonging to a religious order, often working as a missionary or teacher.
Hacendado — owner of a large agricultural estate called a hacienda.
Estanciero — owner or manager of a livestock ranch or rural estate.
Minero — mine owner, administrator, or worker.
Azoguero — specialist or operator involved in processing silver using mercury.
Arriero — mule driver who transported goods between cities, mines, ports, and villages.
Mercader — merchant who bought and sold local or imported goods.
Pulpero — owner of a small shop or tavern called a pulpería.
Artesano — skilled craft worker, such as a carpenter, blacksmith, tailor, or shoemaker.
Maestro de oficio — master craftsman who supervised apprentices and journeymen.
Soldado de presidio — soldier stationed at a fort or frontier garrison.
Marinero — sailor working on commercial or military ships.
Mayordomo — estate, household, or church-property administrator.
Capataz — foreman who supervised laborers on estates, construction sites, or in mines.
